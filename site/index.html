Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies offer a unified approach to combining visual scene understanding with language-guided driving. However, their computational demands make onboard deployment difficult, and their long-horizon closed-loop performance on embedded mobile robots remains insufficiently characterized. Short-horizon trajectory accuracy alone does not establish whether these policies can sustain task execution under onboard sensing and computational constraints.

We present QCarVLA, an embedded driving framework that jointly investigates efficient trajectory generation and sustained closed-loop driving performance. Building on a pretrained driving VLA architecture, QCarVLA adapts visual context to four surround-view cameras and a platform-scaled bird's-eye-view geometry. The trajectory predictor combines autoregressive motion primitive generation with language-conditioned flow-based residual refinement. The compact motion primitive prediction reduces the overhead of autoregressive decoding while continuous refinement from the flow residuals mitigates quantization errors from a finite motion primitive vocabulary.

We also introduce a reproducible benchmark spanning simulation and physical hardware. This benchmark contains multi-stage navigation tasks, traffic controls, and drivable area constraints. A shared recovery-assisted protocol measures behavioral violations and the dependence on expert intervention over extended rollouts. Experiments conducted on the QCar2 platform with an onboard NVIDIA AGX Orin reveal that the QCarVLA policy reduces end-to-end inference latency from 11.46 s to 3.83 s, approximately 3.0× speedup over OpenDriveVLA. These results highlight the computational benefits of compact trajectory representations and establish a common framework for assessing their trade-offs with sustained driving performance.

Benchmark

The benchmark spans 19 multi-stage navigation routes in simulation and on physical QCar2 hardware. Vehicles visit pickup, stop, and drop-off regions in order before returning to the hub. Its common evaluation interface supports vehicles of appropriate scale without prescribing a particular sensor configuration.

  • Held-out evaluation: 13 training routes and six held-out routes; demonstrations use two traffic configurations, with the third reserved for closed-loop testing.
  • Task service: remain within a 0.20 m task region at no more than 0.05 m/s for three continuous seconds.
  • Scoring: traffic and task violations, off-road duration, road-centering error, and dependence on privileged recovery.
  • Rollouts: the scored interval starts at the designated gate; each rollout ends on route completion or a 600-second timeout.

Method

  • Four-camera, platform-scaled perception. The pretrained ResNet-101 image backbone remains frozen. Scene and BEV map tokens are projected into the language backbone, using a 200 × 200 BEV grid spanning ±5 m around the vehicle.
  • Compact motion planning. The Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone autoregressively predicts six 0.5-second motion primitives from a fixed 512-entry vocabulary. Their composition yields 30 future poses over a three-second horizon.
  • Continuous residual refinement. A four-block transformer conditions on six post-primitive hidden states and integrates a residual flow in four Euler steps. The residual adjusts the discrete plan beyond its fixed primitive geometries.
  • Consistency-guided training and deployment. Autoregressive supervision, primitive consistency, and residual flow matching are optimized jointly. The refined trajectory is sampled into six waypoints for an NMPC controller; policy prediction runs asynchronously from vehicle control.
